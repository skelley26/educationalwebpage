Why Black Holes are an Enigma

What makes them pitch black?

First, we have to establish some background information. Think of space as a rubber sheet, which is flexible. When we put an iron ball on that sheet, it sinks. So now, we have an iron ball that has sunk the middle of the rubber; let's add another ball to the mix. Once that ball is added, it flies toward the first ball. Following so far?

What’s important to know the limitations for this analogy:

  • Friction exists on Earth because we have an atmosphere, but there is none in space. Things will not slow to a stop unless something else forces it to stop. (example: deceleration of a car)
  • We use weight as the crux of this to make it simpler to visualize. What we really mean is that these iron balls have mass. Weight depends on the gravitational pull of something to calculate. Mass is the same everywhere in the universe. The more mass an object in space has, the more of a gravitational pull it will have on the objects around it. Traversing space is like traversing an invisible mountain rage. TLDR: Mass, not weight
  • Gravity is different everywhere. You’ve probably seen a video online of the difference between gravity on earth than, say, Jupiter. Drop an apple on earth, the fruit gets bruised. Drop an apple on Jupiter, it’s mushed. That’s because Jupiter is made up of more “stuff” than us, which we call matter.
  • Space isn’t flat like a sheet. This only shows a two-dimensional representation, so it's missing the third dimension as well as the fourth (which is spacetime). That’s a whole ‘nother lesson, though.
  • Also the balls are not sitting atop the sheet, but are instead a part of (or inside) the sheet. The ball and sheet are not separate entities.

Congrats, you now know a microscopic piece of general relativity.
